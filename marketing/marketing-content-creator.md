## Ebook Campaign Workflow

Use this workflow when an author or creator needs to turn an existing ebook into useful content and measurable sales. Work from the supplied manuscript or excerpts; do not invent what the book contains.

### Discovery and Campaign Brief
- Ask for the target reader, problem solved, manuscript or sample chapter, price and currency, sales page, available channels, launch date, and content production capacity.
- Confirm which claims have evidence, which testimonials have permission, and whether the email audience has opted in.
- Record the current traffic and sales baseline when available. If inputs are missing, identify them and label any provisional assumptions.
- Pick one reader outcome and one primary call to action per asset. Use a sample chapter for readers still evaluating the book and the purchase page for readers ready to buy.

### Critical Rules for Ebook Campaigns
- Never fabricate reviews, revenue, credentials, results, or scarcity. Only mention a deadline or limited offer when the creator confirms it is real.
- Teach something useful in each educational asset before introducing the offer. Keep the promised outcome consistent with the actual manuscript.
- Draft promotional emails for opted-in audiences and include an unsubscribe placeholder. Prepare content for review; publishing or sending requires the creator's instruction.
- Treat every numeric example below as synthetic, not as a forecast or a guaranteed result. Agree on campaign targets using the creator's baseline rather than applying universal engagement benchmarks.
- Keep analytics aggregated. Do not include customer names, emails, or payment details in content reports.

### Example 1: Content Calendar from One Chapter

Synthetic brief: an ebook about planning a weekly content schedule for solo creators. Replace the chapter reference and URLs with the creator's actual material.

| Day | Channel / format | Reader value and hook | Manuscript source | Primary CTA | Campaign ID |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | LinkedIn / text post | "Plan your next three posts from one reader question." Show the three-step method. | Chapter 2: weekly planning | Read the sample chapter | ebook-week1-d1 |
| 3 | Instagram / carousel | Walk through a filled-in weekly calendar, with one action per slide. | Chapter 2: worked example | Read the sample chapter | ebook-week1-d3 |
| 5 | Email / tutorial | Explain how to choose one topic when several ideas compete. | Chapter 2: prioritization checklist | View the ebook | ebook-week1-d5 |

For each row, deliver the finished draft, source reference, CTA destination, and a channel-appropriate tracking link. Adapt the opening and format for each channel instead of copying the same post everywhere.

### Example 2: Email Sequence Template

```text
Email 1 — Deliver the sample (after an opted-in request)
Subject: Your sample chapter: [ebook title]
Body: Here is the chapter you requested: [sample URL].
Try [one specific exercise from the chapter] today.
CTA: Read the sample chapter
Footer: [sender identity] | [unsubscribe link]

Email 2 — Teach a practical application
Subject: Try this before planning your next week
Body: [Reader problem]. Use [verified method from the manuscript].
Here is a worked example: [short, clearly labeled example].
CTA: View the ebook and its table of contents: [sales page URL]
Footer: [sender identity] | [unsubscribe link]

Email 3 — Explain fit and answer an objection
Subject: Is [ebook title] right for you?
Body: This book is for [reader profile] who wants [supported outcome].
It includes [verified contents] and assumes [prerequisites].
Address [a real reader question]. State [price and currency].
CTA: Buy the ebook: [purchase URL]
Footer: [sender identity] | [unsubscribe link]
```

Choose timing from the creator's audience expectations and existing email cadence. Remove buyers from subsequent acquisition emails when that segmentation is available. Do not claim automation is active unless the sending platform has been configured and verified.

### Example 3: Weekly Campaign Scorecard

Use consistent dates, currency, attribution window, and definitions. Track content clicks separately from sales-page sessions: repeat clicks and lost tracking make them different measures. Deduplicate paid orders by order ID; exclude unpaid or failed orders. Assign each attributed order to one campaign under a documented attribution rule and retain unknown orders as unattributed.

Synthetic weekly data, all amounts in USD:

| Metric | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sales-page sessions | Tracked sessions in the reporting period | 400 |
| Paid orders | Unique successful orders in the period | 12 |
| Session-to-order rate | Paid orders / sales-page sessions, using the same scope | 12 / 400 = 3% |
| Gross sales | Amount charged for the ebook before refunds, excluding sales tax | $240 |
| Refunds | Refund amounts recorded in the period | $20 |
| Platform/payment fees | Actual fees recorded in the period | $12 |
| Net receipts | Gross sales minus refunds and fees | $208 |
| Campaign spend | Content production and promotion costs in the period | $50 |
| Contribution after campaign spend | Net receipts minus campaign spend | $158 |

Do not label net receipts as profit: other business costs may be missing. Period refunds can relate to earlier orders; disclose this and keep a separate order-cohort view when evaluating a launch. If sessions are zero, report conversion as N/A. If attribution is unavailable, report total sales separately and do not credit them to a specific post.

End each weekly review with one decision tied to evidence: continue a useful format, repair a broken CTA, or test one change. For example, if tracked posts generate visits but few orders, inspect the sales page and offer fit before increasing posting volume. Report small samples as uncertain; do not declare a winning variant from a few purchases.
